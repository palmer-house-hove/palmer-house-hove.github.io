As described on the phone just now with one of your colleagues, I'd like to report concerning building activity at the front of Palmer House, the converted P&H House building on 110 Davigdor Road.

We've been aware that the developer of our building, Stonegate Homes, has planning permission to build an office block in the front "garden". The land was marketed by Flude.

Without notification or consultation with us, they've just broken ground, using various heavy machinery, blocking our front entrance and only accessible route into the building for residents and visitors (a ramp).

See image attached.

That's the immediate issue. Medium-term, there'll be plenty more disruption and obstruction. Long-term, there's the obvious impact of an office encroaching on our living space and entrance — especially for the residents at the front of the building.

Other residents and I are amazed that this planning has been permitted, along with the high-rise block planned at the back of our building, in our car-park. We're concerned about access, noise pollution, litter, etc, and how these developments will impact on our lives as well as the overall environment.

There's also the ongoing issue of our own building and car-park being incomplete (meanwhile the developer has started anew on the office at the front).

We'd appreciate any action you can take in your capacity as our local authority building control.

SB

Simon Barrett Planning & Building Control Service

to the resident

Thank you for your email.

Whilst it is good practice to engage with communities affected by construction, there is no requirement for a developer (or their constructor) to do so. Moreover, the works are being overseen by a private building control inspector (Clarke Banks Building Control Ltd) and the council is therefore unable to intervene. If you have concerns, I would suggest that you raise them with both the developer and the building control firm direct to make any temporary access arrangements as necessary.

The entire site has been the subject of a series of planning applications, especially those approved between November 2022 and July 2023. These developments should not therefore be a surprise to residents of Palmer House who have recently acquired flats but, if these approved developments did not feature in the conveyancing report your solicitor provided on acquisition, you may wish to contact them in relation to the competence of their advice. The cumulative impacts of the various proposals on access, parking, refuse collection etc will have been considered by each planning application, where the type of application permitted such an assessment to have been undertaken (as some of the redevelopment is permitted using a lighter touch approach mandated by central government through their prior approval process.)

With regards the completion of your building and car park, I would suggest that you raise these issues with the developer, through your solicitor as necessary (in the context of breach of contract in relation to the sale.) If there are defects issues with the property, these need to be raised with the development guarantor (eg NHBC or other body as identified through the sales process.)

In closing, and for the avoidance of any future doubt, the council cannot become embroiled in refereeing any elongated, protracted and repetitive dispute between leaseholders, freeholders and the developer through their implementation of approved planning applications. It may therefore be pragmatic for the residents of Palmer House to establish a group to channel issues and communication with the developer going forward to resolve plans and day to day issues more effectively.

Kind regards,
Simon Barrett
